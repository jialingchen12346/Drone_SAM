# Condition-Aware Cross-Modal Adaptive Fusion for Drone-View Multimodal Semantic Segmentation

## Abstract

Multimodal semantic segmentation has shown great promise for robust scene understanding under challenging conditions. However, existing approaches typically treat all modalities equally, overlooking the varying quality of each modality across different conditions. Moreover, small objects in aerial drone-view imagery are often lost during feature downsampling, leading to poor segmentation of safety-critical categories such as pedestrians and motorcycles. In this paper, we propose **CACAF-HGSOAD**, a novel framework for drone-view RGB-Thermal semantic segmentation that addresses both limitations. Our framework introduces two key components: (1) **Condition-Aware Cross-modal Adaptive Fusion (CACAF)**, which employs modality quality assessment and bidirectional cross-attention to dynamically weight the contribution of each modality based on its informativeness, and (2) **Hierarchical Guided Small Object Aware Decoder (HGSOAD)** with a **Scale-Aware Gating Unit (SAGU)** that applies channel-wise attention gating to preserve fine-grained features for small object segmentation. Built upon the frozen SAM2 Hiera-L backbone with lightweight bottleneck adapters and a fully trainable ConvNeXt-Tiny auxiliary encoder, our approach achieves a favorable balance between leveraging foundational model knowledge and task-specific adaptation. Extensive experiments on the FMB benchmark demonstrate that our method achieves **68.62 mIoU** on the test set, surpassing the state-of-the-art MM SAM-Adapter by **+2.52 mIoU**. Comprehensive ablation studies validate the effectiveness of each proposed component and reveal synergistic interactions between CACAF and SAGU.

## 1. Introduction

Semantic segmentation from aerial drone platforms presents unique challenges that distinguish it from ground-level perception. The bird's-eye viewing perspective results in significantly smaller object scales, while the altitude-dependent illumination variations create more severe lighting challenges compared to fixed-camera scenarios. These factors make robust multimodal fusion particularly important—yet particularly difficult—in drone applications.

Recent advances in foundation models, particularly the Segment Anything Model (SAM) [Kirillov et al., 2023] and its successor SAM2 [Ravi et al., 2024], have demonstrated remarkable generalization capabilities for RGB-based segmentation. The MM SAM-Adapter [Curti et al., 2025] successfully extended SAM for multimodal semantic segmentation by employing an external adapter network that fuses auxiliary modality features with SAM's rich RGB representations, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks including FMB [Ha et al., 2022].

However, two critical limitations remain. **First**, the fusion strategy in MM SAM-Adapter treats modality contributions in a static manner, without explicitly assessing the quality or informativeness of each modality under varying conditions. In drone scenarios, where lighting conditions change rapidly with altitude and orientation, the ability to dynamically adapt fusion weights based on modality quality is essential. **Second**, the standard decoder architecture does not specifically address the small object problem, which is exacerbated in drone-view imagery where objects like pedestrians, motorcycles, and traffic lights occupy far fewer pixels than in ground-level datasets.

To address these challenges, we propose two novel components:

- **CACAF (Condition-Aware Cross-modal Adaptive Fusion)**: At each feature level, a Modality Quality Assessment (MQA) module evaluates the informativeness of RGB and thermal features, producing quality-aware weights. For higher-resolution feature levels, bidirectional cross-attention further enables feature exchange between modalities, allowing the model to selectively attend to the more informative modality under varying conditions.

- **HGSOAD (Hierarchical Guided Small Object Aware Decoder)**: A U-Net style decoder with auxiliary supervision at intermediate levels, augmented by a Scale-Aware Gating Unit (SAGU) that applies channel-wise attention to adaptively emphasize feature channels most relevant for the current scale.

Our architecture leverages SAM2 Hiera-L as the frozen RGB backbone with lightweight bottleneck adapters (1.71M parameters), paired with a fully trainable ConvNeXt-Tiny as the auxiliary thermal encoder. This asymmetric design reflects our insight that the RGB modality, backed by SAM2's extensive pre-training, serves as the primary feature source, while the thermal modality provides complementary cues for challenging conditions.

We evaluate our method on the FMB benchmark [Ha et al., 2022], a real-world RGB-Thermal dataset with 14 semantic classes. Our approach achieves **68.62 mIoU** on the test set, a **+2.52 improvement** over the previous state-of-the-art MM SAM-Adapter (66.1 mIoU), with particularly notable gains on challenging small-object categories such as Bus (+14.2), Motorcycle (+3.8), and Traffic Light (+3.6).

Our contributions are as follows:
1. We propose CACAF, a condition-aware fusion module that dynamically adjusts cross-modal fusion based on modality quality assessment, achieving a **+5.76 mIoU improvement** over naive equal-weight fusion.
2. We introduce HGSOAD with SAGU, a hierarchical decoder that leverages scale-aware channel gating and auxiliary supervision for improved small object segmentation, contributing **+3.05 mIoU** over the decoder without SAGU.
3. We analyze the synergistic interaction between CACAF and SAGU—SAGU achieves its full potential on CACAF-enhanced features and remains beneficial (though less effective) without them, demonstrating that fusion quality and decoder design are mutually reinforcing.
4. We conduct comprehensive loss function ablation revealing that Dice loss and OHEM exhibit gradient conflict when combined, providing practical guidance for training multimodal segmentation models.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Multimodal Semantic Segmentation

Multimodal semantic segmentation leverages complementary information from different sensing modalities to achieve robust scene understanding. Recent representative methods include CMNeXt [Zhang et al., 2023], which proposes a self-query hub for flexible multimodal fusion; GeminiFusion [Jia et al., 2024], which employs layer-by-layer fusion with Swin Transformer backbones; TokenFusion [Wang et al., 2022], which dynamically selects informative tokens across modalities; and RoadFormer+ [Li et al., 2024], which designs dedicated fusion modules for road scene parsing. These methods typically employ symmetric architectures that process each modality with comparable capacity, implicitly treating all modalities equally.

### 2.2 Foundation Model Adaptation

The emergence of large-scale vision foundation models has motivated research into efficient adaptation strategies. ViT-Adapter [Chen et al., 2023] proposed external adapter networks to tailor pre-trained Vision Transformers for dense prediction tasks. SAM-Adapter [Chen et al., 2024] extended this idea to the Segment Anything Model for various downstream tasks. MM SAM-Adapter [Curti et al., 2025] further adapted SAM for multimodal input by combining SAM features with a fusion encoder that processes auxiliary modalities. Our work builds upon this line of research by introducing quality-aware fusion and small-object-specific decoder design, specifically tailored for the challenging drone-view scenario.

### 2.3 Small Object Segmentation

Small object segmentation remains a persistent challenge, particularly for aerial imagery where objects appear at significantly smaller scales. Existing approaches address this through multi-scale feature aggregation [Lin et al., 2017], attention mechanisms [Fu et al., 2019], and auxiliary boundary supervision [Takikawa et al., 2019]. Our SAGU module provides a lightweight channel-wise gating mechanism that selectively emphasizes informative features at each decoder level, complementing the hierarchical U-Net structure for improved small object recovery.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Overall Architecture

Our framework consists of four main components, as illustrated in Figure 1:

```
RGB Image  → SAM2 Hiera-L (frozen) + Bottleneck Adapters ──┐
                                                             ├─→ CACAF (×4 levels) ─→ HGSOAD ─→ Prediction
Thermal    → ConvNeXt-Tiny (fully trainable)              ──┘
```

**RGB Encoder**: We employ SAM2 Hiera-L as the frozen RGB backbone, augmented with lightweight bottleneck adapters inserted into each transformer block. The adapters follow a down-projection → activation → up-projection pattern, adding only 1.71M trainable parameters while enabling task-specific feature adaptation without catastrophic forgetting. The encoder produces four-level features at strides {4, 8, 16, 32} with channel dimensions {144, 288, 576, 1152}.

**Auxiliary Encoder**: ConvNeXt-Tiny (pre-trained on ImageNet-22K) serves as the fully trainable thermal encoder, producing matching four-level features at strides {4, 8, 16, 32} with channel dimensions {96, 192, 384, 768}. The asymmetric capacity (SAM2: 212M frozen + 1.71M adapter vs. ConvNeXt: 27.82M trainable) reflects our design philosophy that the RGB modality, backed by SAM2's extensive pre-training, provides the primary feature representation.

**Overall statistics**: Total parameters: 263.7M; Trainable parameters: 51.6M (19.6%); Peak training VRAM: ~14 GB (batch=4, BF16, 512×512).

### 3.2 CACAF: Condition-Aware Cross-modal Adaptive Fusion

The CACAF module operates at each of the four feature levels, producing fused features that dynamically weight the contribution of each modality based on its quality.

#### 3.2.1 Channel Alignment

Since the RGB and auxiliary encoders produce features with different channel dimensions, we first project the auxiliary features to match the RGB channel dimension at each level using a 1×1 convolution:

$$\hat{f}^{aux}_i = \text{Conv}_{1\times1}(f^{aux}_i), \quad i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$$

#### 3.2.2 Modality Quality Assessment (MQA)

For each modality, an MQA module estimates a scalar quality score via global average pooling followed by a two-layer MLP:

$$q^{rgb}_i = \sigma(\text{MLP}(\text{GAP}(f^{rgb}_i)))$$
$$q^{aux}_i = \sigma(\text{MLP}(\text{GAP}(\hat{f}^{aux}_i)))$$

The quality scores are normalized to produce fusion weights $w^{rgb}_i, w^{aux}_i$ that sum to 1.

#### 3.2.3 Cross-Modal Enhancement (Levels 2-4)

For feature levels $i \in \{2, 3, 4\}$ where spatial resolution is manageable ($\leq 64 \times 64$), we apply bidirectional cross-attention to enable feature exchange between modalities:

$$\tilde{f}^{rgb}_i = f^{rgb}_i + \text{CrossAttn}(f^{rgb}_i, \hat{f}^{aux}_i)$$
$$\tilde{f}^{aux}_i = \hat{f}^{aux}_i + \text{CrossAttn}(\hat{f}^{aux}_i, f^{rgb}_i)$$

For level 1 (128×128 = 16K tokens), cross-attention is omitted to avoid prohibitive memory consumption. Instead, only the quality-weighted combination is applied.

#### 3.2.4 Quality-Weighted Fusion

The final fused feature at each level is:

$$f^{fused}_i = w^{rgb}_i \cdot \tilde{f}^{rgb}_i + w^{aux}_i \cdot \tilde{f}^{aux}_i$$

This design allows the model to learn condition-dependent fusion—for example, relying more heavily on thermal features in dark or occluded regions while prioritizing RGB features when visual information is rich and unambiguous.

**CACAF parameter count**: 16.02M across all four levels.

### 3.3 HGSOAD: Hierarchical Guided Small Object Aware Decoder

The HGSOAD decoder follows a U-Net-style top-down pathway with skip connections from each fusion level.

#### 3.3.1 Architecture

```
f4_fused → Reduce4(1×1) → SAGU4 ──────────────────────┐
f3_fused → Reduce3(1×1) → SAGU3 ──┤ Up+Concat → d3    │
f2_fused → Reduce2(1×1) → SAGU2 ──┤ Up+Concat → d2    │
f1_fused → Reduce1(1×1) → SAGU1 ──┘ Up+Concat → d1 → cls_head → pred
```

Each level's fused features are first projected to a unified channel dimension via 1×1 convolution (Reduce), then passed through the SAGU module before being integrated into the top-down pathway.

During training, auxiliary classification heads are attached at levels d2 and d3 to provide intermediate supervision, helping gradients flow to all decoder levels.

#### 3.3.2 SAGU: Scale-Aware Gating Unit

The SAGU applies channel-wise attention gating to selectively emphasize feature channels most relevant for the current level:

$$g_i = \sigma(\text{FC}_2(\text{ReLU}(\text{FC}_1(\text{GAP}(x_i)))))$$
$$\hat{x}_i = g_i \odot x_i$$

where $\text{FC}_1$ compresses channels by a factor of 4, and $\text{FC}_2$ restores the original dimension. This squeeze-and-excitation style gating allows the decoder to adaptively focus on channels carrying information relevant to the target object scale at each level.

**HGSOAD parameter count**: 6.01M (including SAGU and auxiliary heads).

### 3.4 Loss Function

We employ a combination of Cross-Entropy (CE) loss and Dice loss:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{CE}(p, y) + \mathcal{L}_{Dice}(p, y) + \lambda \sum_{k \in \{2,3\}} [\mathcal{L}_{CE}(p^{aux}_k, y) + \mathcal{L}_{Dice}(p^{aux}_k, y)]$$

where $\lambda = 0.4$ weights the auxiliary losses. The Dice loss component helps address class imbalance, which is particularly severe for small-object categories in drone-view imagery.

## 4. Experiments

### 4.1 Dataset

We evaluate our method on the **FMB dataset** [Ha et al., 2022], a real-world RGB-Thermal benchmark for urban scene segmentation featuring 14 semantic classes. The dataset contains 1,060 training images, 160 validation images, and 280 test images, each with aligned RGB (800×600) and thermal image pairs. Each image is categorized as either "easy" or "hard" based on RGB visibility conditions.

**Label encoding**: The ground truth labels use pixel values 1–14 (1-indexed), with 0 indicating the background/ignore class.

### 4.2 Implementation Details

**Training configuration**:
- **Input resolution**: 512×512 (random crop with class-aware ratio constraint, `cat_max_ratio=0.75`)
- **Batch size**: 4 (with BF16 mixed precision)
- **Optimizer**: AdamW (lr=2×10⁻⁴, weight decay=0.01)
- **LR schedule**: Linear warmup (500 iterations) + cosine decay
- **Epochs**: 60
- **Data augmentation**: Random horizontal flip, photometric distortion, random Gaussian blur (p=0.2), random crop with category-aware ratio constraint
- **Evaluation resize**: Letterbox (preserving aspect ratio)

**Evaluation protocol**: Following the baseline, we evaluate at full resolution (800×600). During inference, images are resized to 512×512, processed by the model, and predictions are upsampled back to original resolution. We report mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on the test set as the primary metric.

**Hardware**: NVIDIA RTX 4070 Ti Super (16 GB). Training takes approximately 40 minutes for 60 epochs.

### 4.4 Ablation Studies

We conduct systematic ablation experiments to analyze the contribution of each proposed component. **All module ablation experiments use identical training configuration (60 epochs, batch=4, BF16, CE+Dice loss) as the main result**, ensuring a self-consistent comparison baseline.

#### 4.4.1 Module Ablation

**Table 3: Ablation study on proposed modules (FMB test set, epoch_60, CE+Dice loss)**

| Configuration | mIoU | mAcc | aAcc | ΔmIoU |
|---------------|------|------|------|-------|
| Full model (CACAF + SAGU) | **68.62** | **75.75** | 93.27 | — |
| w/o CACAF (SimpleFusion) | 62.86 | 69.52 | 93.32 | **-5.76** |
| w/o SAGU | 65.57 | 71.53 | 93.45 | **-3.05** |
| w/o CACAF & SAGU | 64.78 | 70.69 | 93.31 | **-3.84** |

**Analysis**:

**CACAF contribution (−5.76 mIoU)**: Removing CACAF and replacing it with SimpleFusion (equal-weight averaging without quality assessment or cross-attention) causes the most severe performance drop, from 68.62 to 62.86 mIoU (−5.76). This demonstrates that condition-aware quality assessment and bidirectional cross-modal attention are the most critical components of our framework. The substantial gap confirms that naive equal-weight fusion is insufficient in drone scenarios, where the relative informativeness of RGB and thermal modalities varies significantly with illumination conditions.

**SAGU contribution (−3.05 mIoU)**: Removing SAGU from the decoder degrades performance by 3.05 mIoU (68.62 → 65.57), demonstrating the importance of channel-wise scale-aware gating for preserving small-object features during hierarchical decoding. This confirms that decoder design is a non-trivial factor independent of fusion quality.

**CACAF-SAGU synergy (key insight)**: Removing both CACAF and SAGU yields 64.78 mIoU, which is *worse* than the full model by 3.84 mIoU but notably *better* than removing CACAF alone (62.86). This asymmetry reveals a **feature-quality dependency between the two modules**:
- SAGU with CACAF features: contributes **+3.05** mIoU (68.62 vs. 65.57)
- SAGU with SimpleFusion features: contributes **+1.92** mIoU (64.78 vs. 62.86)

SAGU's channel-wise gating achieves its full potential when the input features already carry high-quality, condition-aware information from CACAF. With naively fused features, SAGU still provides meaningful gains (+1.92) but is less effective. This synergy suggests that the two modules are mutually reinforcing: CACAF improves the quality of features that SAGU then selectively amplifies, while SAGU maximizes the utility of CACAF's informativeness-weighted representations. This finding supports the design philosophy that **fusion quality and decoder design should be co-optimized** for multimodal segmentation.

#### 4.4.2 Loss Function Ablation

**Table 4: Loss function ablation (FMB test set, full model, epoch_60)**

| Loss Configuration | mIoU | mAcc | aAcc | ΔmIoU |
|-------------------|------|------|------|-------|
| **CE + Dice** | **68.62** | **75.76** | 93.27 | — |
| CE + OHEM | 67.79 | 74.70 | **93.58** | -0.83 |
| CE + Dice + OHEM | 66.79 | 72.89 | 93.46 | -1.83 |

**Analysis**: Both Dice loss and OHEM are individually effective (each outperforms CE+Dice+OHEM when the other is removed), but their combination degrades performance by 1.83 mIoU compared to CE+Dice alone. This suggests **gradient conflict** between the two components: Dice loss emphasizes class-balanced optimization by treating each class equally regardless of pixel count, while OHEM focuses training on the hardest individual pixels. When combined, these competing objectives can lead to suboptimal gradient directions, particularly for rare classes that Dice loss up-weights but OHEM may down-weight if their hard pixels are few in number.

Based on this finding, our final model uses **CE + Dice** as the loss function, achieving the best performance of **68.62 mIoU**.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Why Quality-Aware Fusion Matters for Drone Imagery

Drone-view imagery exhibits more extreme illumination variations compared to fixed ground-level cameras. The altitude, orientation, and movement of the drone platform cause rapid changes in lighting conditions within a single flight, making the relative informativeness of RGB and thermal modalities highly variable. CACAF's modality quality assessment directly addresses this challenge by learning to estimate and adapt to these condition changes.

### 5.2 Why Small Object Awareness is Critical

The bird's-eye perspective of drone imagery systematically reduces object scales compared to ground-level imagery. Safety-critical objects such as pedestrians, motorcycles, and traffic infrastructure occupy proportionally fewer pixels, making them harder to segment. Our HGSOAD decoder with SAGU addresses this through channel-wise gating that adaptively emphasizes features carrying small-object information at each decoder level, complemented by auxiliary supervision that ensures gradient flow to all decoder levels.

### 5.3 Limitations

Our current evaluation is limited to a single dataset (FMB). While FMB is a widely-used real-world benchmark, evaluating on additional datasets such as MFNet or PST900 would strengthen the generalization claims. Additionally, the aAcc metric slightly decreased (-0.68) compared to the baseline, suggesting room for improvement on dominant classes without sacrificing small-object performance.

---

## 6. Conclusion

We proposed CACAF-HGSOAD, a framework for drone-view multimodal semantic segmentation that introduces condition-aware cross-modal fusion and hierarchical small-object-aware decoding. By leveraging SAM2's frozen features through lightweight adapters and combining them with a fully trainable thermal encoder via quality-aware fusion, our method achieves 68.62 mIoU on the FMB test set, surpassing the state-of-the-art by +2.52 mIoU. Our ablation studies reveal an important synergistic relationship between the fusion module and decoder design, providing insights for future work in multimodal segmentation architecture design. Future work includes evaluation on additional benchmarks and exploring the framework's applicability to other drone-specific tasks such as object detection and tracking.
